# Nicoletti v The State of Western Australia [2026] WASCA 115

## One line
Non-consecutive transcript page numbers given to the jury did not create a reasonable apprehension of bias or a miscarriage of justice.

## What happened
NICOLETTI was convicted on 26/08/2025 in the District Court (MASSEY DCJ) of one count of sexual penetration without consent contrary to s 325 Criminal Code (WA). The count was one of several on a larger indictment; an earlier trial hung on various counts and a later trial of this count was aborted after the jury retired. At the trial that convicted him, the jury got a 'dummy' indictment with only this count and no mention of the earlier proceedings. The complainant's edited recorded evidence (given 19/02/2024) was played, and the jury received a transcript of witness evidence. The transcript pages were not consecutive — witness evidence ran pages 4121–4195 and the complainant's ran 1076–1113 — reflecting editing and the broader proceedings. The trial judge told the jury the numbering would be out and there would be gaps due to editing. NICOLETTI appealed, arguing the page numbers signalled he had been involved in extensive criminal proceedings, raising a reasonable apprehension of bias amplified by media coverage.

## What the Court held
The Court of Appeal refused leave and dismissed the appeal, finding the ground had no reasonable prospect of succeeding. Three reasons: (1) the practice of numbering transcript pages consecutively across all proceedings under one indictment is not known to an ordinary juror or fair-minded member of the public, so the numbers communicated nothing about other charges; (2) the reasoning the appellant attributed to the fair-minded lay observer was mere speculation with no objective basis in fact; and (3) it would require the jury to disregard the trial judge's clear directions (not to speculate, decide only on the evidence, transcript is not evidence) and their oaths. Applying HCF v The Queen [2023] HCA 35, an apprehension must have an objective basis in fact, and violation of the juror's oath is not lightly to be inferred; jurors are assumed to understand and conform to directions.

## What it means for your casework
This is a conviction-appeal ruling with little direct operational impact, but it reinforces useful points. Courts strongly presume juries follow directions and honour their oaths, so claims of prejudice from incidental material need a concrete factual foundation, not speculation. Where a matter involves multiple counts, aborted or hung trials, and media coverage, prosecutors use tools like a 'dummy' indictment and editing to keep extraneous material from the jury — be alert to keeping your brief clean of prior-proceeding references. Incidental artefacts like non-consecutive transcript page numbers were held not to convey prejudicial information to lay jurors. The test for post-verdict apprehension of bias from jury issues comes from HCF: whether a fair-minded, informed member of the public might reasonably apprehend the jury did not decide according to law, on the evidence, and per directions.

## Verdict
AWARENESS only — this turns on its own facts and changes nothing in how you investigate or charge; it confirms the strong presumption that juries follow directions.

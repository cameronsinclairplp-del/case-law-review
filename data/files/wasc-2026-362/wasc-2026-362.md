WHITBY J:
Introduction and overview
	On 24 December 2025, the appellant was sentenced in the Perth Magistrates Court on his plea of guilty to a total effective sentence of 12 months' imprisonment for four counts of burglary contrary to s 401(2)(c) of the Criminal Code (WA).
	The appellant seeks to appeal his sentence on the following four grounds:
(1)	the sentencing magistrate made an express error of law by finding the appellant was on bail for burglary when he committed one of burglary offences;
(2)	the sentencing magistrate made an error of law in failing to give any weight to relevant mitigatory factors;
(3)	the sentencing magistrate failed to properly consider whether to suspend the sentence of imprisonment which resulted in a miscarriage of justice; and
(4)	the sentence imposed was excessive as to type having regard to all of the circumstances of the offending and the appellant's personal circumstances, especially his time in custody.
	The appellant applied to this court for bail, pursuant to s 7F(1)(b) of the Bail Act 1982 (WA) pending the determination of his application for leave to appeal and appeal against his sentence. On 3 June 2026, I granted the appellant bail. He was released from custody to bail on the same day.
	The respondent opposes the appeal.
	For the following reasons, I grant leave to appeal and allow the appeal on ground 1. As this requires me to resentence the appellant, there is no need to consider grounds 2 to 4 of the appeal.
	I resentence the appellant to a total effective sentence of 8 months' imprisonment. The appellant has spent 8 months and 6 days in custody for these offences. He has therefore served his full sentence.
The facts and circumstances of the offending and sentencing
	The facts and circumstances of the relevant offending were, in summary, as follows.
	On the evening of 4 August 2025, the appellant approached the Reece Plumbing centre in Osborne Park wearing a face covering and used tape to cover the CCTV camera. The appellant used a screwdriver to remove the door hinge pins and entered the store. He walked around the store, picked up a cash register key, before activating the security system and running from the store. The incident was captured on CCTV (PE/47107/2025).
	A short time later, that same evening, the appellant used a crowbar to pry open the door of the Coffee Cabinet in Osborne Park. The appellant walked behind the counter, forced open a cash register, and stole $500 in cash from the register. As he ran from the store, he dropped four $10 notes. CCTV captured the appellant walking away from the Coffee Cabinet (PE/47109/2025).
	Less than a week later, on 9 August 2025, the appellant broke into the Folk and Merchant Café in Doubleview by unknown means and stole an unknown amount of cash from a cash register (PE/47111/2025).
	The appellant was arrested on 10 August 2025 and charged with the above offences. He was granted and released on bail.
	While on bail, on 21 September 2025, the appellant forced entry into the Copper Chimney Fine Indian Cuisine in South Fremantle and stole a tip jar containing $226 and an OscarPOS order taking tablet valued at $260 (FR/9294/2025).
Sentencing of the appellant
	The appellant pleaded guilty to the offences on 24 December 2025. By this time the appellant had spent three months in custody.
	The sentencing magistrate characterised the appellant's offending as not highly sophisticated but noted that it must have involved some degree of premeditation given he wore a face covering on one occasion and was armed with a crowbar on another.
	Her Honour considered the following matters in sentencing the appellant:
(1)	the offending was born out of desperation and was likely an attempt to obtain funds to purchase drugs - her Honour stated this was neither an aggravating nor mitigating factor;
(2)	the primary objectives for offending such as this were specific and general deterrence;
(3)	the appellant had good prospects for employment and, despite his extensive criminal history, had previously demonstrated he was able to be offence free for periods of time;
(4)	the appellant was entitled to a 25% reduction in sentence for his guilty pleas, pursuant to s 9AA of the Sentencing Act 1995 (WA); and
(5)	the appellant had expressed genuine remorse for his offending.
	The sentencing magistrate imposed the following sentences for each of the offences:

	The appellant was sentenced to a total effective sentence of 12 months' immediate imprisonment backdated to commence on 25 September 2025 and the appellant was made eligible for parole.
	The sentencing magistrate declined to conditionally suspend the term of imprisonment. Her Honour said this was because the number of offences, together with the clear need for specific and general deterrence, meant that a term of immediate imprisonment was the only appropriate outcome.
	The appellant appeals his sentence on the four grounds I have outlined. He does not appeal against the convictions.
Appeal ground 1: material error of fact
	The sentencing magistrate made an error of fact in stating that the appellant was on bail when he committed the Copper Chimney Burglary on 21 September 2025 and the Folk and Merchant Café Burglary on 9 August 2025. However, the appellant was not charged with the Coffee Cabinet Burglary and the Reece Plumbing Burglary until 10 August 2025. Therefore, the appellant did not commit the Folk and Merchant Café Burglary while he was on bail, contrary to what the sentencing magistrate found.
	The respondent accepts that the sentencing magistrate made this error. However, the respondent says that the error was not material, because it would not have resulted in the imposition of a different sentence had the sentencing magistrate correctly recognised that the Folk and Merchant Café Burglary was not committed while the appellant was on bail.
	Although the appellant did commit the Copper Chimney Burglary whilst he was on bail for three charges of burglary, the commission of an offence whilst on bail is a serious aggravating factor. In my view, this error is one which is capable of affecting the total effective sentence which was imposed.
	As a result, it is necessary for this court to resentence the appellant. The appellant's counsel made submissions in relation to the appellant's current personal circumstances and informed the court that it otherwise had been provided with all of the necessary material to conduct that resentencing.
Resentence
	The appellant will be 48 years old on 30 August 2026.
	At the time of these offences, the appellant was experiencing significant mental health and substance misuse issues. He was living in his car. He made full admissions to the police when he was arrested for the Copper Chimney Burglary offence, telling the police that he had broken into the restaurant because he was starving and had no food.
	The appellant has been offered a permanent position as a rigger which would require him to abstain from illicit substance use and undergo testing to ensure he does. The appellant now has stable accommodation with a friend.
	The appellant is genuinely remorseful for his offending and pleaded guilty to the offences at the earliest opportunity for which he is afforded a 25% discount.
	While the appellant has an extensive criminal record, there are significant periods of time when he is offence free. He has been compliant with his current bail conditions.
	There is a need for general deterrence in relation to these types of offences which are inherently serious. There is also the need for personal deterrence given the appellant committed four burglary offences in a short period of time and the last of the offences was committed while he was on bail for the other three offences.
	The maximum penalty for each of the offences is 2 years' imprisonment.
	I must first fix an appropriate sentence for each offence and then consider questions of cumulation and concurrence, taking account of the totality principle.
	Having regard to all of the circumstances of the offending, the aggravating and mitigating factors and the maximum penalty, I consider that:
(1)	for the Reece Plumbing Burglary, 6 months' imprisonment;
(2)	for the Coffee Cabinet Burglary, 2 months' imprisonment;
(3)	for the Folk and Merchant Café Burglary, 2 months' imprisonment; and
(4)	for the Copper Chimney Burglary, 2 months' imprisonment.
	In my view, the total criminality of the offending warrants a total effective sentence of 8 months' immediate imprisonment. The Reece Plumbing Burglary is the head sentence, the Copper Chimney Burglary sentence is to be served cumulatively and the remaining offences are concurrent. The sentence is backdated to commence on 25 September 2025. Given the appellant has served in excess of 8 months in custody, he has served his full sentence. The effect of this is that he will serve no further time in custody.

# hca-2026-10 — The King v AR [2026] HCA 10

VERDICT: PROBLEMS

- **whatHeld** — "the tendency is an intermediate fact proved to a lesser (civil) standard unless it is an indispensable link to guilt" — The Court did not fix the standard as the 'civil standard' (balance of probabilities). The joint reasons describe it only as a 'lesser standard' governed by 'the principles applicable to circumstantial evidence', and the other judgments expressly characterise it as an indeterminate standard rather than the civil standard. Labelling it '(civil)' adds a precision the Court declined to commit to. The judgment says: "[8]: 'the standard of proof concerning such evidence is governed by the principles applicable to circumstantial evidence. Unless the tendency is an indispensable intermediate step in reasoning to guilt then... the jury should not be instructed that... it must be satisfied that those acts have been proved beyond reasonable doubt.' Gordon and Steward JJ ([85]) refer to satisfaction 'on an indeterminate standard of proof'."
